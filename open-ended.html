How to use this page

Write before you reveal

For every numerical problem, show the formula or conversion-factor chain, substitute values with units, calculate, and report the final answer with a unit. For explanation questions, write at least two complete sentences and use chemistry vocabulary.

Suggested 4-point rubric: 1 point for correct concept or equation, 1 for setup and units, 1 for correct result or claim, and 1 for reasoning or interpretation. A mathematically correct answer with no setup should not receive full credit during practice.
Section 1

Foundation short response

MixturesQ1

A clear sample contains water, dissolved sodium chloride, and a small amount of sand at the bottom. Classify the sample as homogeneous or heterogeneous and explain your choice.

Check reasoning

Expected: Heterogeneous overall because the sand creates more than one visible phase. The salt-water portion is homogeneous, but the entire sample is not uniform throughout.

MixturesQ2

Two students disagree about air. One calls it a pure substance because it looks uniform. The other calls it a homogeneous mixture. Which student is correct? Explain using composition.

Check reasoning

Air is a homogeneous mixture because it contains multiple gases that are uniformly mixed. Uniform appearance does not make something a pure substance.

Physical/Chemical ChangeQ3

A cold soda is opened and bubbles form rapidly. Does bubbling alone prove that a chemical reaction occurred? Explain.

Check reasoning

No. Dissolved carbon dioxide can escape when pressure decreases, which is a physical process. Gas formation can be evidence of chemical change only when a new gas is produced by a reaction.

Physical/Chemical ChangeQ4

Classify each as physical or chemical and justify one of your classifications: melting candle wax, burning candle wax, crushing chalk, tarnishing silver.

Check reasoning

Melting wax: physical. Burning wax: chemical. Crushing chalk: physical. Tarnishing silver: chemical. A valid justification identifies whether chemical identity changes and whether new substances form.

IsotopesQ5

An atom has 15 protons, 16 neutrons, and 15 electrons. Identify the element, give its mass number, and write its isotope name in words.

Check answer

Atomic number 15 is phosphorus. Mass number = 15 + 16 = 31. The isotope is phosphorus-31.

IsotopesQ6

Explain why carbon-12 and carbon-14 are the same element even though they have different masses.

Check reasoning

Both have 6 protons, and proton number determines elemental identity. They differ in neutron number, so they are isotopes of carbon.

Electromagnetic RadiationQ7

Visible red light has a longer wavelength than visible violet light. Compare their frequencies and photon energies without calculating.

Check reasoning

Red light has lower frequency and lower photon energy. Violet light has shorter wavelength, higher frequency, and higher photon energy.

Dimensional AnalysisQ8

Explain why multiplying by 1000 is correct when converting 3.2 kg to grams, but dividing by 1000 is correct when converting 3200 g to kilograms.

Check reasoning

There are 1000 g in 1 kg. Dimensional analysis shows 3.2 kg × 1000 g/1 kg, while 3200 g × 1 kg/1000 g. The orientation of the conversion factor is chosen so the starting unit cancels.

Section 2

Quantitative free response

DensityQ9

A mineral has a mass of 86.4 g. Water in a graduated cylinder rises from 35.0 mL to 46.2 mL when the mineral is submerged. Determine the mineral's density.

Solution

Volume = 46.2 − 35.0 = 11.2 mL. Density = 86.4 g ÷ 11.2 mL = 7.71 g/mL.

DensityQ10

A liquid has density 1.18 g/mL. What volume contains 44.3 g of the liquid? Show the rearranged equation before calculating.

Solution

V = m/D = 44.3 g ÷ 1.18 g/mL = 37.5 mL.

Density + GeometryQ11

A metal cube has an edge length of 2.50 cm and a mass of 132.8 g. Calculate its density.

Solution

Volume = (2.50 cm)³ = 15.625 cm³. Density = 132.8/15.625 = 8.50 g/cm³ to three significant figures.

Density + PredictionQ12

A plastic sample has mass 18.6 g and volume 22.0 cm³. Calculate its density and predict whether it floats or sinks in water with density 1.00 g/mL.

Solution

D = 18.6/22.0 = 0.845 g/cm³. Since 1 cm³ = 1 mL and the sample is less dense than water, it should float.

ConversionQ13

Convert 0.00645 km to centimeters using a dimensional-analysis chain.

Solution

0.00645 km × 1000 m/1 km × 100 cm/1 m = 645 cm.

Two-unit ConversionQ14

Convert 18.0 m/s to km/h using dimensional analysis.

Solution

18.0 m/s × 1 km/1000 m × 3600 s/1 h = 64.8 km/h.

Density + ConversionQ15

A substance has density 2.70 g/cm³. What mass, in kilograms, would occupy 450 cm³?

Solution

m = DV = 2.70 × 450 = 1215 g = 1.22 kg to three significant figures.

EM RadiationQ16

Calculate the frequency of electromagnetic radiation with wavelength 4.80 × 10⁻⁷ m. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s.

Solution

ν = c/λ = (3.00 × 10⁸)/(4.80 × 10⁻⁷) = 6.25 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

EM RadiationQ17

Radiation has frequency 9.50 × 10¹⁶ Hz. Calculate its wavelength in meters.

Solution

λ = c/ν = (3.00 × 10⁸)/(9.50 × 10¹⁶) = 3.16 × 10⁻⁹ m.

IsotopesQ18

An element has two naturally occurring isotopes: isotope A has mass 62.0 amu and abundance 69.0%; isotope B has mass 64.0 amu and abundance 31.0%. Calculate the average atomic mass.

Solution

(62.0 × 0.690) + (64.0 × 0.310) = 42.78 + 19.84 = 62.62 amu.

IsotopesQ19

An element's average atomic mass is much closer to 35 amu than to 37 amu. Without calculation, what can you infer about the relative natural abundances of its 35 and 37 isotopes?

Check reasoning

The 35 amu isotope must be more abundant. A weighted average lies closer to the mass of the isotope with the larger fractional abundance.

Section 3

Reasoning, error analysis, and lab thinking

Error AnalysisQ20

A student calculates density as volume ÷ mass and reports 0.250 mL/g. The sample mass is 40.0 g and volume is 10.0 mL. Identify the error and give the correct density.

Solution

The formula was inverted. Density = mass/volume = 40.0 g/10.0 mL = 4.00 g/mL.

Error AnalysisQ21

A student writes 4.6 m × (100 m / 1 cm) to convert meters to centimeters. Explain what is wrong and correct the setup.

Solution

The conversion factor is reversed and numerically incorrect. Use 4.6 m × 100 cm/1 m = 460 cm. The meters cancel.

Lab ReasoningQ22

You need to determine the density of an irregular metal bolt using a balance and graduated cylinder. Describe the measurements and calculation you would perform.

Expected response

Measure the bolt's mass on the balance. Record initial water volume, submerge the bolt fully, and record final volume. Bolt volume = final − initial. Density = mass/bolt volume.

Lab ReasoningQ23

In a density experiment, a student reads the water level from above instead of at eye level. Explain how this could affect the measured volume and why that matters for density.

Expected response

Viewing from the wrong angle can create parallax error and produce a volume that is too high or too low. Since volume is in the denominator of D = m/V, that error changes the calculated density.

Physical/ChemicalQ24

A white solid is heated. It melts, then later turns brown and releases a gas. Separate the observations into evidence of physical change and possible evidence of chemical change.

Expected response

Melting is physical because it is a phase change. The new brown color together with gas release may indicate chemical change because new substances may have formed.

MixturesQ25

Design a physical separation strategy for a mixture of iron filings, sand, and dissolved salt in water. Give the steps in a sensible order and explain the property used at each step.

Expected response

Use a magnet to remove iron based on magnetism. Filter to remove insoluble sand based on particle size/solubility. Evaporate the water to recover dissolved salt based on volatility/boiling behavior.

EM RadiationQ26

Two electromagnetic waves travel through vacuum. Wave A has twice the frequency of Wave B. Compare their wavelengths quantitatively.

Expected response

Because c = λν and c is constant, wavelength is inversely proportional to frequency. If A has twice B's frequency, A has half B's wavelength.

IsotopesQ27

A student says, "If an isotope has more neutrons, it must be a different element." Explain precisely why this statement is incorrect.

Expected response

Element identity depends on proton number, not neutron number. Changing neutrons changes the isotope and mass number while leaving the element unchanged, as long as the proton number stays the same.

Section 4

Mixed Honors challenge

Multi-stepQ28

A rectangular metal bar measures 4.00 cm × 1.50 cm × 0.800 cm and has a mass of 42.8 g. Calculate its density and decide whether it could be aluminum, whose density is approximately 2.70 g/cm³.

Solution

Volume = 4.00 × 1.50 × 0.800 = 4.80 cm³. Density = 42.8/4.80 = 8.92 g/cm³. It is not consistent with aluminum.

Multi-stepQ29

A sample has density 0.790 g/mL. What mass in grams would fill a 1.50 L bottle? Show the volume conversion first.

Solution

1.50 L × 1000 mL/L = 1500 mL. m = DV = 0.790 × 1500 = 1.19 × 10³ g or 1190 g.

Multi-stepQ30

An electromagnetic wave has wavelength 2.00 × 10⁻⁶ m. A second wave has wavelength 5.00 × 10⁻⁷ m. Calculate both frequencies and state which has greater photon energy.

Solution

ν₁ = 1.50 × 10¹⁴ Hz. ν₂ = 6.00 × 10¹⁴ Hz. The second wave has greater photon energy because it has higher frequency.

Data InterpretationQ31

Three trials for the density of the same metal give 7.81, 7.79, and 9.15 g/cm³. What should the student notice before averaging the values? What would you do next?

Expected response

9.15 g/cm³ is inconsistent with the other two and may be an outlier caused by measurement or recording error. The student should inspect that trial, repeat the measurement if possible, and justify whether it should be excluded rather than automatically averaging all three.

Argument from EvidenceQ32

A student claims an unknown liquid is water because it is colorless. Another student measures 25.0 mL and finds a mass of 19.8 g. Evaluate the first student's claim using quantitative evidence.

Solution

Density = 19.8/25.0 = 0.792 g/mL. That is substantially below water's approximate density of 1.00 g/mL, so color alone is weak evidence and the density measurement does not support identifying the liquid as water.

SynthesisQ33

Write your own two-step Chemistry Honors problem that requires both a unit conversion and the density equation. Then solve it completely.

Self-check criteria

Your problem should provide enough information, require a genuine unit conversion, use D = m/V or a rearrangement, show unit cancellation, and end with a physically reasonable answer.

SynthesisQ34

Write a short explanation connecting these three ideas: atomic number, isotope, and average atomic mass. Your explanation should make clear why the periodic-table atomic mass is often not a whole number.

Expected response

Atomic number identifies an element by its proton count. Isotopes of that element have the same proton count but different neutron counts and therefore different masses. The periodic-table atomic mass is a weighted average of naturally occurring isotope masses, so it is often not an integer.
